
WORKING TITLE
Trade-Flow Imbalance and Short-Horizon Market Impact:
Evidence from High-Frequency Equity Data

1. INTRODUCTION
- Motivation: understanding how trading activity relates
  to short-horizon price movements.
- Research question: Does normalized trade-flow imbalance
  predict subsequent equity returns?
- Contribution: empirical comparison across eight stocks,
  multiple return horizons, and out-of-sample prediction.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
- Kyle (1985): market depth and price impact.
- Cont, Kukanov, and Stoikov (2014): order-flow imbalance.
- Almgren and Chriss (2000): optimal execution.
- Empirical research on transient and persistent impact.

3. DATA AND METHODOLOGY
- LOBSTER-derived high-frequency equity observations.
- Eight stocks: AXP, BA, CAT, CSCO, CVX, GS, MMM, VZ.
- Minute-level aggregation.
- Signed trading volume and normalized imbalance.
- Next-minute returns measured in basis points.
- OLS regression with Newey-West HAC inference.
- Multi-horizon analysis: 1, 2, 5, and 10 minutes.
- Chronological out-of-sample directional evaluation.

4. EMPIRICAL RESULTS
- Positive estimated imbalance coefficients.
- HAC significance across all eight stocks.
- Modest explanatory power.
- Directional accuracy above majority-class baseline.
- Cross-stock differences and horizon sensitivity.

5. ROBUSTNESS AND LIMITATIONS
- Outlier sensitivity.
- Alternative HAC lag lengths.
- Trading-session boundary validation.
- Potential omitted variables and endogeneity.
- Distinction between trade-flow and order-book imbalance.
- Need to validate the precise return horizon definitions.

6. CONCLUSION
- Summary of preliminary predictive relationships.
- Implications for short-horizon price-impact modeling.
- Future work: event-level order impact and
  additional stocks.
